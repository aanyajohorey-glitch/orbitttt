# Timed user study: a creative brief with ORBIT vs usual tools

**Question:** does ORBIT help people put together a cohesive creative direction faster, with fewer
tools, and feel satisfied with it?

## Design
- **Participants:** 6-10 classmates who make things (posters, videos, music, writing). Teacher
  permission; voluntary; initials only (P01, P02, ...).
- **Task:** build a one-page creative brief for a given prompt (for example *"a poster for the school
  music night"*): a concept, 5 colours, 2 fonts, 3 visual references, 5 songs.
- **Two conditions, same person:** once with their **usual tools** (Pinterest, Coolors, Google Fonts,
  Spotify, search, and so on) and once with **ORBIT**. Use a different prompt each time, and swap the
  order for half the participants so practice doesn't favour one condition.
- **Measure:**
  - `minutes` until the brief is done (stop at 30 minutes);
  - `tools_used`: number of different apps or sites;
  - `satisfaction`: 1-5 ("I'm happy with this brief");
  - `cohesion`: 1-5 ("everything feels like one world"), rated by a second person who doesn't know
    which condition it was.

## Recording
Copy `data/user_study_template.csv` to `data/user_study.csv` (it stays on this computer). One row per
participant per condition. The AI Lab shows the averages for each condition.

## Reporting honestly
- With under 10 people, report averages and the range, and avoid words like "proves".
- Note what could bias the result: people who like ORBIT may rate it higher; ORBIT's suggestions come
  from small, hand-labelled catalogues; time limits favour fast tools.
